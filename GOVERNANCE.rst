Pele Suite Governance
=====================

The Pele Suite is led in an open governance model that is described in this document.

Role
^^^^

Members of the steering committee (SC) can change organizational settings of the software, do administrative operations such as rename/move/archive repositories, change branch protection rules, etc.

SC members can call votes for decisions or requests for input from technical, governance or advisory groups.

The SC can veto decisions of the technical committee (TC) by a majority vote within the SC.
The technical committee (TC) can override an SC veto with a 2/3rd majority vote.
The SC can change the governance structure by unanimous SC vote.

SC decisions are documented in developer meeting notes and/or on the GitHub repository.


Decision Process
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Decisions of the SC usually happen in the developer meetings, via e-mail or public chat.

Decisions are made in a non-confidential manner by the majority of votes cast by SC members.

SC votes can be cast asynchronously, e.g., over a time period of 1-2 weeks.
In tie situations, the chair of the SC serves as the tie breaker.

Appointment Process
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

A chair and new members of the SC can be appointed by unanimous vote of the current SC members.

SC members are expected to attend and contribute to regular developer meetings.

SC members can resign, and they can be removed by majority vote, e.g., due to inactivity, inappropriate or otherwise negative behavior, or other reasons.

Role
^^^^

The technical committee (TC) is the core governance and action body, where, under normal operations, most ideas regarding the direction of the Pele Suite are discussed and decisions are made about implementation priorities and strategies.
TC members are valuable, vetted developers of The Pele Suite, with contributions that include, but are not limited to, actual code and/or algorithms, code pull requests, supporting input for issues & user discussions, writing and updating documentation and tutorials, visualizations, testing and benchmarking, gathering poll data from the advisory panel, etc.
TC members are expected to attend and contribute to regular developer meetings.
TC members can "triage" (i.e., add and remove labels to) pull requests, issues, and GitHub discussion pages and comment on and review PRs.
TC members are expected to seek approval of another maintainer for their own changes, except under exigent circumstances.
TC members lead and weigh in on technical discussions and, if needed, can call for a vote of the TC for a technical decision.
TC members can merge/close PRs and issues, and moderate (including blocking or muting) bad actors.
The TC can propose governance changes to the SC.


Decision Process
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Discussion in the TC usually happens in the developer meetings or by email. Developer meetings can be proposed by any member of the SC or TC, and should be announced to the entire SC and TC by the TC chair with sufficient lead time to maximize attendance.

If a member of the TC calls for a vote, the vote will be decided by the majority of the votes cast, provided that at least half of the TC members participate in the vote. If fewer than half of the TC members cast votes, the SC will make the decision.

Votes are cast in a non-confidential manner. Decisions are documented in the developer meeting notes maintained in the GitHub repository.

Individual TC members can suggest actions by the TC. The suggestion must be seconded by another TC member. Any TC member has the right to oppose the suggestion and call for a vote of the TC on the decision.

Appointment Process
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

New TC members can be suggested by either SC or TC members. Confirmation of a new TC member requires a majority of votes cast by either the SC or the TC. The SC can veto any new appointment.

SC members can also be TC members.

TC members can resign or be removed by majority vote by either TC or SC due to inactivity, inappropriate or otherwise negative behavior, or other reasons.

Role
^^^^

Members of the advsiory panel (AP) are encouraged to provide regular recommendations to the TC for the future directions of the Pele Suite, in terms of suggested infrastructure capabilities, physical/modeled processes incorporated within the Suite, validation/verification activities, etc, to maximize the scientific and industrial impact of the software.

The SC and TP will regularly poll the AP via Email 1-2 times per year for updates to recommended Pele development activity. Any recommendations by the AP should be shared publicly and documented in GitHub. The TP should respond to the AP to report on the feasibility of suggestions, plans for implementation, and a summary of the status of previous suggestions.

Decision Process
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Input from the AP is usually gathered via e-mail or public chat and forwarded to the TP for consideration and response (see above).

Group input from the AP is made in a non-confidential manner by the majority of votes cast by AP members. Such input should be addressed by the TP, as outlined above.

AP input can be gathered asynchronously, e.g., over a time period of 1-2 weeks.


Appointment Process
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

New members of the AP can be appointed by unanimous vote of the current AP members.

AP members are expected to respond to emailed polls for input that are initiated by other members of the AP and by the TC.

AP members can resign or can be removed by majority vote, e.g., due to inactivity, inappropriate or otherwise negative behavior, or other reasons.



Former Members
--------------

Former (e.g., inactive) members of the SC, TC and AP do not play any role in the governance of The Pele Suite and are not maintained on the roster.

Instead, former members are acknowledged separately in GitHub contributor tracking, Pele Suite documentation, references, etc. as appropriate.
